Reading this as: an identity for an open-source command-line prompt-review tool — an instrument, not a mascot — in a letterpress-and-ink register, leaning scientific-editorial.

Silverquill — three marks

The name is chosen. The old mark was a literal nine, so the mark is being made from scratch. Three directions, each carrying a different idea about what the product actually does.

Dials — design variance 6, motion intensity 1, visual density 3. An identity has no motion; the page keeps its own. Palette is the page's existing token set: ground #151417, ink #f0eee9, signal #d4ed80. Type is the page's existing pair: Fraunces for the wordmark, Geist for utility settings.

Direction one

The Rule

A rubric is a ruled line with marks, and the run stops at the ninth. The mark is that line, with the ninth mark tall. It carries the old name's number into the new name's instrument without keeping the number as a wordmark.

Silverquill
Small scale

My read: the strongest idea, and the only one that explains the old name instead of forgetting it. Its problem is scale — at sixteen pixels the tenth of a rubric disappears, so the favicon needs a simplified variant (six marks, one tall). It also asks the reader to know what a rubric is before they know the product.

Direction two

The Quoted

The product's signature act is quoting: it names the exact line that is wrong, with a quote, then fixes it. The mark is that quote, drawn as two precise hooks — the product's own behaviour, abstracted.

Silverquill
Small scale

My read: the best behaviour-match and the best small-scale behaviour — two strokes read at any size, and it needs no simplification for the favicon. Its cost is that a pair of quote marks is a general typographic symbol; on its own it is not distinctive, and it does not carry the word "quill".

Direction three

The Nib

The name is a writing implement, so the mark is one: a nib and the stroke it has just written. Silver and ink, one stroke, no ornament.

Silverquill
Small scale

My read: the prettiest and the most obviously the name. Its danger is that a nib is the oldest mark in the book — a fountain pen reads as heritage, and this product argues for the opposite. It also leans a little costume, which is the one register the page has avoided so far.

Direction files live in brand/silverquill/marks/. The wordmark sets in Fraunces for editorial surfaces and in Geist for terminal and package surfaces; nothing is drawn as a raster except the social card, so a swap costs one file each way.
